HumanProof

I write, therefore I am.

The Tables of the Law

For a keyboard that knows a human typed, without ever saying who.

The seven laws

  1. II. Biometrics never leave the keyboard.Only the signed verdict does; the measurements are born and die in the chip.
  2. IIII. Anonymous by default.It proves “a genuine keyboard”, never “this keyboard”.
  3. IIIIII. Identity only by choice.A separate key for each service, impossible to link from one to another.
  4. IVIV. A refusal is never an accusation.Cold fingers, tremors, disability: “not sure”, never “robot”.
  5. VV. Never mandatory.No one is shut out for dictating, writing with their eyes or typing differently.
  6. VIVI. Open.A public format that any manufacturer can produce and anyone can verify, without a platform.
  7. VIIVII. Proof of humanity is not a right.It grants no authority over the reader’s machine.

Don’t prove who you are. Prove that you are there.

Preamble

Soon, no one online will be able to tell a human from a machine. Machines write as we do, imitating our style, our punctuation, our mistakes. CAPTCHAs are falling one after another.

Behavioural signals, typing rhythm or mouse movements, are measured on the machine of the very person being judged: if it lies, they lie with it. A measurement taken on the imitator’s side proves nothing.

The Keyboard that Attests

Liveness sensors

Rhythm, where the finger lands, force, temperature and the finger’s micro-tremor. One signal can be imitated; all of them together, far less easily.

The sealed key

Born in the keyboard’s chip, it never leaves it, and the manufacturer certifies it.

The seal of the text

The keyboard signs what it typed, with a challenge from the recipient. Change one letter and the signature fails. Pasted text is not certified.

It is not proof of who drafted the words; it is proof that a human typed them and stands behind them, like a signature at the bottom of a contract. A fingerprint on what we write.

Beyond the keyboard

Mouse, camera, microphone: each sensor can sign what it captures, with its own liveness detection. Their common limit: whatever is replayed in front of the sensor, a screen or a loudspeaker, passes for real. The keyboard escapes it better: a finger must press the key. So we start there.

The Call

E-mail was born in 1982 with no proof of sender; it took until 2007, and billions of spam messages, to add one. Let us not repeat the mistake with AI: prove that a human is there before opening the floodgates. Every past attempt foundered on privacy. If proof of humanity becomes mandatory, let it be in this form and no other.

Ideas belong to those who bring them to life.
