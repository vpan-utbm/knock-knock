# HumanProof

> ## I write, therefore I am.

Soon, no one online will be able to tell a human from a machine. Machines write as we do. CAPTCHAs are
falling. A measurement taken on the imitator's side proves nothing.

**Don't prove who you are. Prove that you are there.**

## The keyboard that attests

- **Liveness sensors in the keys**: rhythm, where the finger lands, force, temperature, the finger's
  micro-tremor.
- **A key born in the keyboard's chip**, which never leaves it.
- **The keyboard signs what it typed**: the hash of the text, the "human finger" verdict, a challenge from
  the recipient. Change one letter and the signature fails. Pasted text is not certified.

It is not proof of who drafted the words; it is proof that a human typed them and stands behind them. Like a
signature at the bottom of a contract. A fingerprint on what we write.

## Seven principles

1. **Biometrics never leave the keyboard.**
2. **Anonymous by default**: it proves "a genuine keyboard", never "this keyboard".
3. **Identity only by choice.**
4. **A refusal is never an accusation.**
5. **Never mandatory**: no one is shut out for writing differently.
6. **Open**: verifiable by anyone, without a platform.
7. **Proof of humanity is not a right**: it grants no authority over the reader's machine.

## Beyond the keyboard

Mouse, camera, microphone: each sensor can sign what it captures, with its own liveness detection. Their common
limit: whatever is replayed in front of the sensor, a screen or a loudspeaker, passes for real. The keyboard
escapes it better: a finger must press the key. So we start there.

## The call

Every past attempt foundered on privacy. If proof of humanity becomes mandatory, let it be in this form and
no other.

Manufacturers, build it. Platforms, accept anonymity. Legislators, protect those who cannot type.

**Ideas belong to those who bring them to life.**

— *Human After All*, 13 November 2026
